My plan vs. what actually happened

For each ticket: what I expected, what the agent team actually did, and what I took away from it. Jump to a ticket:

Differences by ticket

Ticket 101

Bulldog tee for Tauhid Zaman

Different from my plan

What I expected

A sale that had already gone through but was running late. The team would confirm the purchase, check that it shipped, and let Tauhid know it was on the way.

What actually happened

There was no sale on record and size S was out of stock. The agents sent Tauhid an honest status update, and Accounting prepared the supplier's $840 bill for the tee reprint (the restock) for me to approve. I approved it.

My reflection

What actually happened was different from what I expected. I assumed this was an earlier sale that had gotten stuck in the warehouse or hadn't been ordered properly. Instead, the agents worked together and figured out that we were out of stock on this exact item. They then lined up the order for more stock and brought it to me to approve. It felt seamless, and I appreciated the work they did. My one piece of feedback: the first time through, it was confusing what I was being asked to approve. It didn't make clear that I was paying for more stock of this product. Once the approval screen spelled that out, it made sense.
Ticket 102

Rent notice from Elm City Properties

Matched my plan

What I expected

Facilities confirms we owe the rent, Accounting updates the books and sets up the payment, and the Boss reports back to me.

What actually happened

Facilities matched the notice to lease 1 ($2,400 due September 2) and confirmed it wasn't already paid. Accounting prepared the payment, and once I approved it the payment was recorded and checking dropped to $160.

My reflection

This ticket ran just as I expected. The system found the rent on our lease, confirmed it was the right amount, and prepared the payment for me to approve. It ran smoothly.
Ticket 103

Yale AI Club bulk hoodie order

Not what I planned

What I expected

Inventory confirms we have the hoodies and updates stock, Accounting records the sale and creates an invoice, and Customer Service tells the club the next steps.

What actually happened

Only 8 of the 20 hoodies were in stock, and we have no discount policy on record, so the team put the request on hold. There was no sale, no invoice and no stock change, and the club was asked for its timing and discount details. About $1,160 of sales at list price walked away.

My reflection

I was surprised by how this ticket played out. This was a large order, and the system turned it down. Going forward, I need to give the agents better directions so we handle an order like this differently. I'm still surprised that we turned down such a large order.

What would have been simpler as one agent with tools, and why?

Ticket 102 Ticket 102 was simple enough for one agent to handle. All we needed to do was pay our rent, which didn't call for extra agents. Bringing in more of them made the process take a little longer and created duplicate work.

From the run: Accounting was called twice, once by the Boss and once by Facilities. Between them, the agents looked up the checking balance 3 times and pulled the same lease and payment records more than once, for 12 steps in 23 seconds. One agent holding the documents and checking-account tools could have done it in a fraction of that.

New problems this can solve

  1. Real-time cash flow. The checking balance updates every time cash moves, so I can see right away whether we have enough on hand for what's coming due.
  2. A clear record of where our cash went. The Cash tab lays out each transaction and its effect, what added to our cash and what drew it down, so I can explain the ending balance at a glance.
  3. A simpler way to run the business. I watched the Accounting agent work out cost of goods sold, margins and gross profit on its own. Having that done for me makes everyday decisions easier.

Problems it can't solve yet

  1. Large or unusual orders. I'm still surprised by how the agents handled ticket 103. We lost a big order because it was more complex than the rules we gave them. Next, I'd build a tool for special orders above a certain size, so they get a real path forward instead of a no.
  2. Out of stock and short on cash. We need a solution for when an item is out of stock and we don't have the cash to restock it. I'd add a tool that lets an agent request a purchase on a credit card or another form of credit, still with my approval.
  3. Marketing. Growing the business matters too. I'd add a marketing agent with tools that drive customer outreach.
